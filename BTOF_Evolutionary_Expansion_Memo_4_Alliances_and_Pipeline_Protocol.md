# Expansion memo 4: alliances, and the protocol for an A*-only pipeline (2 Oct 2026)

## 1. Alliances: what I said, what I left out, and why

You did not miss it. Alliances appear three times in memos 1 to 3, each time in passing: as a "seventh, smaller option" in memo 1, as pipeline #6 scored 8 in memo 2, and as a word in the firm-variation cell of the memo 3 matrix with no study attached. No alliance paper was ever placed above 8. Three reasons, two of them defensible and one not.

- **Pre-emption, defensible.** The direct link from performance feedback to alliance behavior is already published in good places. Kavusan and Frankort 2019 SMJ did a behavioral theory of alliance portfolio reconfiguration. Greve, Baum, Mitsuhashi and Rowley 2010 AMJ and Greve, Mitsuhashi and Baum 2013 Org Sci did feedback-driven alliance withdrawal. Tyler and Caner 2016 SMJ did below-aspiration product introductions and R&D alliances. The plain "gap predicts alliance formation" paper is taken.
- **Data, defensible.** SDC alliance coverage is incomplete and uneven, which Schilling 2009 SMJ documented, so the evidence score is capped at 2 for anything where alliances are the dependent variable.
- **Anchoring, not defensible.** I took your own 7-10 score for #6 as the ceiling for the whole alliance line and never re-asked the question under the evolutionary frame. Under that frame alliances are not a referent. They are the borrowed-variation channel, the one way an incumbent can adapt to a replacing technology without inventing it, and the evolutionary alliance literature is older and thinner than the behavioral one: Koza and Lewin 1998 Org Sci on the co-evolution of alliances, Rothaermel 2001 SMJ on incumbents exploiting complementary assets through cooperation with biotech entrants, Lavie and Rosenkopf 2006 AMJ on balancing exploration and exploitation across alliance function, structure and attribute.

## 2. Building an A*-only pipeline: what I need and how to run it

### 2A. The diagnosis you made is right

The 20-paper list carried conditional contributions. Idea #2 was 11 if the two search types diverge and 7 if not. Idea #5 was 11 if the diagnosis predicts direction and 8 if not. A contribution that needs a particular sign is not a contribution, it is a bet. The A* test I would apply from now on has four parts, and an idea must pass all four before any data are touched:

1. **Two-literature question.** The question is one that two literatures both care about and neither can answer alone. Adaptation vs selection is the template.
2. **Sign-proof contribution.** The paper is interesting whichever way the result goes. Write both abstracts before building. If one of them is boring, the idea fails.
3. **Identification reviewers accept now.** An instrument, a shock, or a within-firm design that an SMJ or AMJ reviewer in 2026 will not reject in the first paragraph. The examiner lottery, delisting codes, and wave timing are yours.
4. **One borrowed, one new.** Either the construct or the outcome is new to the field. Both borrowed is an extension; both new is unpublishable.

Applied to the current candidates, the sign-proof test sorts them as follows.

| Passes all four now | Fails sign-proof as stated | Fails identification as stated |
|---|---|---|
| N1 search and survival; N2 problem-born explorations; N3 pruning; N8 replacement and response; A5 gross vs net; A6 diagnostic feedback; N9 last to leave | A7 barbell, which needs the U-shape; #7 localization, which needs the localization effect; #2 input vs output; #5 attribution | A4 symbolic gap-closing, until the transactions file gives the leniency link at the application level; N10, until the response-mode choice has an instrument |

The second and third columns are not dead. A7 becomes sign-proof if it is reframed as "where on the distance spectrum does feedback move resources," with the barbell as one of three named patterns. #7 becomes sign-proof if the question is "does the firm search where the problem is or where the capability is," with both answers theorized.

### 2B. What I need from you, in order of value

1. **The master CSVs, three to five fields, one at a time.** The right fields are the ones that border the program: BTOF (your 187), organizational ecology and Red Queen, exploration-exploitation and incumbent adaptation to technological change, alliances, and patent-based search and recombination. If a sixth exists for organizational learning from failure, add it. The columns that matter, in order: title, authors, year, journal, abstract, theory label, IV constructs, DV constructs, moderators, data source, sample window, method, key finding. If the CSV only has metadata and abstracts, it is still usable. If it has the IV, DV and operationalization columns, it is three times more useful, because cross-field ideas come from juxtaposing operationalizations, not topics. Do not send the PDFs. Abstracts and tables are enough and they avoid putting copyrighted text in a repository.
2. **The audited Data Center variable dictionary.** The list of built variables with definitions, windows and sources. Without it I am guessing which ideas are ready and which need a build, and readiness decides the order.
3. **The rejection letters and reviewer reports.** SO desk reject, JMS reject on P023, Technovation and IJOA on P009, Alexy's reject-and-resubmit on P017. These say what the field refused to count as a contribution, which is the fastest way to calibrate the sign-proof test to real reviewers rather than to me.
4. **Your constraints.** Hours per week for new builds through Aug 2027, which coauthors exist for which lines, and which methods you will not use. Hazard models, text measures and instruments each cost weeks the first time.

### 2C. The procedure, once those arrive

1. **Construct map per field.** For each CSV, a table of IV constructs by DV constructs, with counts, moderators, data sources and methods, and the empty cells marked. One field per session.
2. **Juxtaposition.** Constructs rich in one field and absent in another, where the second field's outcome is measurable on your disk. Each juxtaposition is a candidate. This is where the cross-disciplinary ideas come from, and it needs at least three fields to work, because two fields give you only one boundary.
3. **Pre-emption check.** A targeted search of 2023 to 2026 for each candidate before it is scored. Memo 3 did this for the population ideas; it has to be done for every survivor.
4. **The four-part test.** Both abstracts written. Identification named. The borrowed and the new element named. Anything that fails goes to a parked list with the reason.
5. **Scoring and stopping rules.** Your 3C+E+B score, with the stopping rule written before the first regression, as you did for P026.
6. **Build order.** By score, then readiness, then by what each paper builds for the next one. N1 and N2 build the pieces N8 needs.

Three to five fields is the right number. Two is too few for pattern recognition across boundaries. More than five dilutes attention and the later fields add less. Start with BTOF, then ecology, then alliances, since those three already have papers waiting in this memo.
