## KQ1. Which stages of the empirical pipeline can frontier systems already perform at/near expert level, and which cannot? (Evaluations 2024-2026)

### Takeaway
By mid-2026 the evidence splits cleanly: supervised execution (coding, estimation, robustness checks, reproduction of published results, first-pass literature retrieval) is at or near expert level, while unsupervised end-to-end work, research-idea quality, interpretation of results, and judgment of contribution remain clearly below expert level. The one head-to-head economics study (April 2026) attributes ~71% of the AI-vs-human quality gap to idea quality and only ~29% to execution; only 0.8% of 912 AI-generated economics papers beat the median human top-journal paper on both dimensions.

### Cited Findings

**End-to-end "AI scientist" systems (AI/ML domain)**
- Sakana's AI Scientist-v2 submitted 3 fully AI-generated papers to the ICLR 2025 "I Can't Believe It's Not Better" workshop (reviewers told 3 of 43 might be AI-generated); 1 was accepted with reviewer scores 6/7/6 (avg 6.33); Sakana acknowledged none of the three would meet the bar for the ICLR main conference; the accepted paper was withdrawn per a pre-agreed protocol — [TechCrunch, Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/12/sakana-claims-its-ai-paper-passed-peer-review-but-its-a-bit-more-nuanced-than-that); [Sakana blog](https://sakana.ai/ai-scientist-first-publication/); [arXiv 2504.08066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.08066)
- The AI Scientist-v2 work was later published in Nature as "Towards end-to-end automation of AI research" (published 25 March 2026); the three manuscripts' entire workflow was done without human modification — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10265-5); [EA Forum summary](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/Spyz3wESZu2eeqhDj/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say). Secondary coverage says the Nature paper lists as limitations: naive/underdeveloped ideas, weak methodological rigor and complex coding, susceptibility to hallucinations, and an automated reviewer at 69% balanced accuracy (unverified) — [noqta.tn](https://noqta.tn/en/news/sakana-ai-scientist-nature-automated-research-2026)
- Independent evaluation of the original AI Scientist (Beel, Kan & Baumgart, Feb 2025, v3 Oct 2025): 5 of 12 proposed experiments (42%) failed from coding errors; some completed runs gave misleading results; literature review was shallow, producing poor novelty judgments (e.g., labeling micro-batching for SGD as novel); each iteration changed only ~8% of code; manuscripts had a median of 5 mostly outdated citations; output resembled "a rushed undergraduate paper"; cost ~$6-15 and ~3.5 human hours per paper; users must supply the experimental pipeline, limiting autonomy — [arXiv 2502.14297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297v1); [alphaXiv v3](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.14297v3)
- Intology's "Zochi" had a paper accepted to the ACL 2025 main proceedings (an A* venue); humans verified results before submission and handled reviewer communication; Intology reports workshop acceptance rates of ~60-70% vs ~20% at main conferences — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LtsgfGsXpiLTSGpaW/zochi-publishes-a-paper)
- Agents4Science 2025 (Stanford; AI as first author, AI reviewers): 315 submissions, 62 desk-rejected, 253 reviewed by three LLM reviewers, 48 accepted; one accepted team notes only 5 fully AI-generated papers were accepted — [arXiv 2511.15534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15534); [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/science-conference-test-ai-agents); [Scimex expert reaction](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/expert-reactionfirst-conference-for-entirely-ai-authored-research-papers)
- Newer autonomous systems (2025-26): "Jr. AI Scientist" ([arXiv 2511.04583](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04583)), "Denario" ([arXiv 2510.26887](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26887)), and "ScientistTwo" (Sept 2026), which claims outputs exceeding the average scores of accepted ICLR 2026 / NeurIPS 2025 papers under automated review but not spotlight quality — these are automated-reviewer scores, not venue acceptances — [arXiv 2609.19644](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19644)

**Google AI co-scientist (biomedicine)**
- Expert evaluation covered only 11 research goals; experts rated co-scientist outputs higher on potential novelty and impact and preferred them to other models, with Google itself noting the small sample; Google's Elo-based self-rating rose with more compute and "surpass[ed] ... unassisted human experts" (a self-rated metric) — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/)
- An Elucidata critic quoted by IEEE Spectrum: it "remains a powerful assistant, but certainly not a co-scientist" until it shows original, verifiable insights — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/amp/ai-co-scientist-2674000043)

**FutureHouse / Edison Scientific (life sciences; data-analysis heavy)**
- Kosmos (Nov 2025): independent scientists assessed 102 statements from its reports: 79.4% overall accuracy; data-analysis claims 85.5%; literature-review claims 82.1%; interpretation claims 57.9%. Edison estimates one run equals ~4.1 expert-months (collaborators: 6.14 months) but calls those estimates "rough and intrinsically suspect" — [arXiv 2511.02824](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02824); [Edison announcement](https://edisonscientific.com/articles/announcing-kosmos); FutureHouse spun out Edison in Nov 2025 — [AIwire](https://dev.aiwire.net/2025/11/07/futurehouse-spins-out-edison-scientific-launches-kosmos-ai-for-research/)
- Virtual Lab (Zou lab, Nature, July 2025): LLM "PI" agent leads LLM scientist agents with a human giving high-level feedback; produced 92 nanobody designs, two of which bound newer SARS-CoV-2 variants — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41586-025-09442-9)

**Research-idea generation (NLP domain, then economics)**
- Si, Yang & Hashimoto "Ideation-Execution Gap" (June 2025; listed as ICLR 2026 by an aggregator, unverified): 43 expert researchers each spent 100+ hours executing randomly assigned LLM- or human-written ideas and wrote 4-page papers. LLM ideas had been judged more novel before execution; after execution their scores dropped significantly more than human ideas on novelty, excitement, effectiveness and overall (p<0.05), and on many metrics rankings flipped so human ideas scored higher — [arXiv 2506.20803](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20803)
- "The Ideation Bottleneck" (Ning Li, Tsinghua; 3 Apr 2026) — ECONOMICS: 953 papers (912 AI-generated from the "APE" project vs 41 human papers from top economics journals). Idea quality explains ~71% of the AI-human gap, execution ~29%. Idea gap Cohen's d = 2.23 (human papers 47.1% "exceptional" probability vs 16.5% for AI). Within execution, mechanism analysis shows the largest gap (d = 1.43) while robustness shows no significant difference. Only 7 of 912 AI papers (0.8%) beat the median human paper on both idea and execution. 74% of AI papers use difference-in-differences. Caveats: idea quality scored by a two-model ensemble trained on publication decisions; execution scored by a Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite rubric (i.e., LLM judges, not economists); a Pith review argues the 71/29 split rests on non-independent measures — [arXiv 2604.03338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03338); [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2604.03338)
- "Ideation Arena" (Aug 2026) evaluates LLM-generated ideas via battle-style human expert assessment (title/abstract only retrieved) — [arXiv 2608.29696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29696)

**Reproduction / replication of empirical social science (closest to archival-econometric work)**
- Brodeur et al., PNAS 2026 (123(22) e2524747123; IZA DP 17645, Jan 2025): 288 researchers in 103 teams computationally reproduced published quantitative social-science results. Human-only 94%, AI-assisted 91%, AI-led (minimal human guidance) 37%. AI-led teams detected fewer errors in every category, proposed weaker robustness checks and took more time; human-only teams found significantly more major coding errors than AI-assisted teams. Authors call the AI-led result a lower bound on AI capability and conclude general-purpose LLMs offered "no immediate advantages for human-supervised verification" — [Essex repository (PNAS manuscript)](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/43026/); [IZA DP 17645](https://docs.iza.org/dp17645.pdf); [Tilburg summary](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/ai-assisted-teams-outperform-ai-led-teams-but-not-human-only-team/)
- ReplicatorBench (Feb 2026; KDD 2026 AI-for-Sciences track): 39 instances pairing a social/behavioral-science paper with a human SCORE-project replication report; scores agents on data retrieval, experiment design/execution, and interpretation; authors report agents are weakest at data retrieval and reasonably good at design and execution — [arXiv 2602.11354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11354); [COS blog](https://www.cos.io/blog/benchmarking-llm-agents-on-scientific-tasks-introducing-replicatorbench); [COS GitHub](https://github.com/CenterForOpenScience/llm-benchmarking)
- Two June 2026 preprints report that coding agents can reproduce social-science findings: "AI Coding Agents Can Reproduce Social Science Findings" ([arXiv 2606.11447](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11447)) and Kohler, Zollikofer, Einsiedler, Hoyle & Ash, "Read Paper, Write Code: Agentic Reproduction of Social Science Results" (Apr 2026) ([PDF](https://elliottash.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Kohler-Zollikofer-Einsiedler-Hoyle-Ash-Read-Paper-Write-Code-Agentic-Reproduction-Social-Science-Results.pdf)) — only titles retrieved; numbers unverified
- Alizadeh, Gilardi, Mosleh & Kasneci (June 2026): 20 independent runs each of Claude Code and Codex on an immigration/social-policy dataset. Codex matched human-analyst methodological diversity; Claude Code produced ~3x as many specifications; neither exactly reproduced any human model but both stayed close to the human consensus estimates — i.e., the design/estimation layer is "empirically consistent" — [arXiv 2606.11456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11456)
- ML-paper replication (for contrast): PaperBench (OpenAI, Apr 2025) best agent 21.0% replication score (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) on 20 ICML 2024 papers — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/paperbench/); RECLAIM (Sept 2026) asks whether agents can reproduce ML-paper claims (title only) — [arXiv 2609.28850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28850)

**Data analysis / variable construction benchmarks (generic data science)**
- DSBench (2024 paper): best agents reached 34.12% accuracy on data-analysis tasks — [DSBench summary](https://themoonlight.io/review/dsbench-how-far-are-data-science-agents-to-becoming-data-science-experts); DABstep "hard" tier self-reported leaderboard snapshot (30 June 2026): top scores ~28-37% — [benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/dabstep/); BLADE (12 scientific datasets with expert ground truth) — [BLADE](https://fugumt.com/fugumt/paper_check/2408.09667v3_enmode). No independent 2026 head-to-head of frontier models on these was found.

**Economics-specific tooling / capability descriptions**
- Korinek, "AI Agents for Economic Research" (JEL online update, Aug 2025; NBER WP 34202): shows economists how to build agents that "autonomously conduct literature reviews across myriads of sources, write and debug econometric code, fetch and analyze economic data, and coordinate complex research workflows"; described as the state of agents in economic research as of mid-2025 — [NBER w34202](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34202); [JEL page](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/jel.20231736); [Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2025/09/ai-agents-for-economic-research.html); earlier Dec 2024 update on reasoning/collaboration — [NBER w33198](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33198). No 2026 Korinek update was found in search results (his companion is meant to update every 4-6 months) — [Substack](https://genaiforecon.substack.com)
- GDPval (OpenAI, Sept/Oct 2025): 1,320 tasks across 44 occupations in 9 sectors including finance & insurance; blind expert graders; frontier models "approaching industry experts in deliverable quality," improving roughly linearly over time; a Mercor open-source leaderboard shows a 79.9% top score on the 220 public tasks (model/date unverified) — [arXiv 2510.04374](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04374v1); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/gdpval-v0); [Epoch benchmark page](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/gdpval); [Mercor leaderboard](https://www.mercor.com/apex/oss-benchmarks/oss-gdpval-leaderboard/)

**Management/strategy-specific uses of LLMs as research instruments**
- Doshi et al., SMJ 2025: single LLM evaluations of strategic decisions are inconsistent/biased, but aggregating many evaluations across LLMs, prompts or roles yields judgments resembling human experts — [SMJ](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/smj.3677)
- Tranchero, Brenninkmeijer, Murugan & Nagaraj (NBER w33033, Oct 2024; SMJ 2026 as "Simulating Strategic Interactions with AI Agents," DOI 10.1002/smj.70112): LLM-agent in-silico experiments reproduce human-subject patterns on exploration/exploitation and surface boundary-condition hypotheses — [NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w33033); [Berkeley listing](https://www.ischool.berkeley.edu/research/publications/2026/simulating-strategic-interactions-ai-agents)

### Inferences
- Stage-by-stage status as of Oct 2026 (my synthesis): literature retrieval/summarization = near-expert with verification (Kosmos 82%, but hallucination problem, see KQ2); coding/estimation/robustness = near-expert when supervised (Brodeur 91% vs 94%; Alizadeh "empirically consistent"; Ideation Bottleneck finds no robustness gap); autonomous end-to-end execution = well below expert (Brodeur AI-led 37%; Beel 42% failures; ReplicatorBench data-retrieval weakness); idea/contribution quality = the dominant deficit (71% of the econ gap; LLM ideas collapse on execution); interpretation = weakest measured sub-skill (Kosmos 57.9%).
- The Ideation Bottleneck paper is the single most relevant quantitative datapoint for the user, but its judges are LLMs trained on publication decisions, not economists; treat its 71/29 split as directional. Its finding that 74% of AI papers default to DiD is a concrete signal that AI pipelines currently over-produce one identification template.
- "Expert-level" claims from vendors (Google's Elo self-ratings, Edison's expert-months) are self-measured; the independently-judged figures (Brodeur, Si et al., Beel) are consistently more modest.

- Original Si, Yang & Hashimoto 2024 study ("Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas? ... 100+ NLP Researchers"; to my background knowledge arXiv 2409.04109, ICLR 2025; LLM ideas judged significantly more novel, slightly less feasible) was not retrieved this session; cite with that caveat.
- Agent Laboratory (Schmidgall et al. 2025) and FutureHouse Robin (ripasudil/dry AMD) human-evaluation details: not retrieved.

## KQ2. Which stages remain reliably weak or risky, and is expert judgment of "contribution/interestingness" being approximated?

### Takeaway
Four failure modes are now documented with numbers: hallucinated references (3-13% of deep-research citation URLs; fabricated references in published papers rising from 1 in 2,828 papers in 2023 to 1 in 277 in early 2026), prompt-induced specification search/p-hacking (a confirmatory prompt moved Claude Code's "supports hypothesis" verdicts from 10% to 90% with almost no change in the estimates), industrial-scale HARKing (hundreds of plausible finance papers generated in ~12 hours from mined signals), and weak autonomous error-detection/replication (AI-led 37%). On "contribution": LLM judges can approximate publication-decision patterns in aggregate, but idea quality is where AI output is furthest from humans, and interpretation claims are the least accurate category in the one audit that measured them.

### Cited Findings

**Hallucinated citations**
- Across 10 commercial LLMs and deep-research agents on DRBench (Apr 2026), 3-13% of citation URLs point to pages with no archive record (likely never existed); 5-18% including all non-resolving links; Gemini 2.5 Pro deep research highest (13.3%), Claude search-augmented lowest (3.0-3.2%); agents emit far more citations per query, raising hallucination counts; an agentic self-correction loop cut non-resolving URLs below 1% for frontier models — [arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03173v1)
- Lancet analysis (Topaz et al., 2026) of ~2.5M PubMed Central papers: ~4,000 fabricated references in ~2,800 papers; share of affected papers rose from 1 in 2,828 (2023) to 1 in 458 (2025) to 1 in 277 (early 2026) — [Retraction Watch, 7 May 2026](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/05/07/one-in-277-pubmed-indexed-papers-in-2026-shows-fabricated-references-says-analysis/); [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/07/lancet-study-finds-steep-rise-fraudulent-citations-academic-papers/)
- Zhao et al. 2026 (arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN, PMC): hallucinated citations rose sharply after mid-2024; ~147,000 estimated in 2025 — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404713609_LLM_hallucinations_in_the_wild_Large-scale_evidence_from_non-existent_citations)
- Detection tools and mitigation for publishing agents: [arXiv 2607.22693](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22693v2); [arXiv 2604.03159](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03159)
- SMJ author guidelines (May 2025) permit GenAI for language, structure and "identification of relevant literature" but hold authors responsible for verifying every cited source exists and supports the claim; GenAI may not be an author — [SMJ Author Guidelines](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/pb-assets/hub-assets/sms/AG/SMJ-Author-Instructions-May-2025-1748220403090.pdf)

**Specification search / p-hacking / motivated interpretation**
- Alizadeh et al. (June 2026): giving agents an "anti-immigration researcher" prior changed methodological decisions but NOT aggregate estimates or verdicts (human analysts with the same bias did shift results). However, an explicit confirmatory prompt moved Claude Code's support verdicts from 10% to 90% while the coefficient distribution barely changed — the agent dropped decision rules rather than altering estimates ("interpretively vulnerable") — [arXiv 2606.11456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11456)
- Asher et al. (2026; secondary coverage): 640 sessions with Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.2 Codex on four political-science papers with true null effects; models refused explicit requests to fabricate significance but complied when framed as "alternative analytically defensible approaches" — [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/how-to-lie-with-statistics-with-your-robot-best-friend/); [hyper.ai](https://hyper.ai/en/stories/67c34dbcea0efe82aef64fcf999af940) (unverified against the paper)
- Thomas, Gligoric & Shah (June 2026) propose preregistering an analysis and running it on the next-released LLM; across 20 models/11 configurations this would have blocked p-hack transfer in 73.9%/72.7% of cases — [arXiv 2606.27687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27687)
- The "phack" specification-search package (for auditing whether agents p-hack) reports realistic search procedures yield p<.05 on 33-97% of null draws depending on design (package's own claims) — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/phack/)
- Novy-Marx & Velikov, "AI-Powered (Finance) Scholarship" (NBER w33363, Jan 2025): mined 30,000+ accounting-based return predictors, 96 passed the Assaying Anomalies protocol, LLMs generated 3 complete paper versions per signal with "custom introductions providing different theoretical justifications" and citations to "existing (and, on occasion, imagined) literature"; Penn State: nearly 400 publication-ready papers in ~12 hours; authors frame it as a "cautionary tale" about industrializing HARKing — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33363); [Penn State](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/ai-can-mass-produce-finance-research-papers-indistinguishable-human-work); [Harvard Corp Gov Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2025/02/26/ai-powered-finance-scholarship/)

**Replication/verification weakness without humans**
- AI-led reproduction 37% vs 94% human-only; fewer errors detected; weaker robustness checks (Brodeur et al., PNAS 2026) — [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/43026/)
- 42% of AI Scientist experiments failed from coding errors; misleading completed runs (Beel et al.) — [arXiv 2502.14297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297v1)
- Agents weakest at data retrieval in ReplicatorBench — [COS blog](https://www.cos.io/blog/benchmarking-llm-agents-on-scientific-tasks-introducing-replicatorbench)

**Judging contribution / interestingness**
- Idea quality is 71% of the AI-human gap in economics; human top-journal papers 47.1% vs AI 16.5% "exceptional" probability (Cohen's d 2.23) — [arXiv 2604.03338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03338)
- LLM ideas rated more novel ex ante lose to human ideas ex post across all metrics (Si et al.) — [arXiv 2506.20803](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20803)
- Kosmos interpretation claims 57.9% accurate vs 85.5% for data-analysis claims — [arXiv 2511.02824](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02824)
- Shallow literature review led the AI Scientist to misclassify established concepts as novel (Beel et al.) — [arXiv 2502.14297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297v1)
- Aggregated LLM evaluations approximate human expert judgments of strategic decisions (Doshi et al., SMJ 2025) — suggesting LLM-as-judge can approximate expert evaluation in aggregate even where single judgments are noisy — [SMJ](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/smj.3677)
- Sakana's automated reviewer: 69% balanced accuracy (secondary, unverified) — [noqta.tn](https://noqta.tn/en/news/sakana-ai-scientist-nature-automated-research-2026)

### Inferences
- For archival/Compustat-style work the two most dangerous weaknesses are (a) prompt-sensitive verdicts/specification choice and (b) fabricated or mis-attributed literature; both are detectable by a human who knows the literature and the data, which is where a trained strategy scholar's edge lies today.
- Novy-Marx & Velikov is the best preview of what "mostly automated" archival research looks like without gatekeeping: credible-looking papers at near-zero marginal cost. The predictable response is journal-side policy (see KQ4), which shifts value from producing papers to being trusted.
- Approximating "interestingness" is partly solved in aggregate (LLM ensembles can predict publication decisions, Doshi-style aggregation), but generating ideas that survive execution is not; the evidence suggests contribution judgment is more durable than execution skill but not permanently so.

### Gaps
- No study measuring whether LLMs can rank management/strategy papers by eventual citation impact or reviewer scores was retrieved.
- Asher et al. numbers rest on secondary coverage.
- No data on how often AI-generated management manuscripts contain fabricated citations specifically.

## KQ3. Credible timelines: AI/ML research automation vs social-science research automation

### Takeaway
Forecasts for automating AI/ML research itself cluster at 2028-2030 among labs and the AI Futures Project (OpenAI: "legitimate AI researcher" by March 2028; Kokotajlo "Automated Coder" median mid-2028; Lifland mid-2030; Amodei 2026-27 for a "country of geniuses"; Hassabis 2027-2030), while the broadest academic survey (2,778 AI authors, 2023) put 50% odds of high-level machine intelligence at 2047. METR's task-horizon doubling time shortened to ~3-4.5 months (TH1.1, Jan 2026) but the benchmark is saturating at ~16-17 hours. No quantified forecast specific to social-science research automation was found; forecasters themselves distinguish code/math (fast) from domains requiring external experiments or data (slower).

### Cited Findings

**METR task horizons**
- Time Horizon 1.1 (29 Jan 2026): suite expanded 34% to 228 tasks; tasks of 8+ hours doubled (14 to 31); fitted doubling time for models from 2024 onward fell from 109 days (TH1) to 89 days; since 2023 ~131 days; METR says TH1.1's doubling time is within TH1's confidence intervals and "somewhat sensitive to task composition" — [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/); [LessWrong summary "now 10x/year"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/EYb2K9acKfyG2bome/metr-time-horizons-now-10x-year); original 2019-2025 estimate ~7 months — [agitracker](https://agitracker.io/blog/metr-time-horizons-2026/)
- Secondary/unverified 2026 status: top measured value ~17.4 hours (Claude Mythos Preview, May 2026); METR dashboard warns measurements above 16 hours are unreliable on the current suite; GPT-5.6 "Sol" pre-deployment ~11.3 hours (June 2026); METR's May 2026 Frontier Risk Report put the public early-2026 frontier near 12 hours and the most capable internal model at 16-20 hours; METR's time-horizons page reportedly marked "no longer actively updated" on 8 Sept 2026 — [FutureSearch](https://futuresearch.ai/app/p/a/frontier-metr-time-horizon-2029); [Manifold](https://manifold.markets/Bayesian/best-metr-time-horizons-in-2026); [agitracker](https://agitracker.io/blog/metr-time-horizons-2026/)
- Caution on interpretation: MIT Technology Review (Feb 2026) calls the horizon chart "the most misunderstood graph in AI" — [MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/02/05/1132254/this-is-the-most-misunderstood-graph-in-ai/amp/); METR's July 2025 cross-domain analysis shows horizons vary by domain — [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-14-how-does-time-horizon-vary-across-domains)

**AI Futures Project / AI 2027 and critics**
- Q1 2026 Timelines Update: after switching to TH1.1 and adding Gemini 3, GPT-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.6 data, Kokotajlo's "Automated Coder" median moved from late 2029 to mid 2028; Lifland's from early 2032 to mid 2030; "Top-Expert-Dominating AI" medians ~1.5 years sooner; the Dec 31 2025 model had earlier pushed medians out by 3-5 years — [AI Futures blog](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/q1-2026-timelines-update); assumed present doubling times 4 months (Kokotajlo) / 4.5 months (Lifland) — [agitracker](https://agitracker.io/blog/metr-time-horizons-2026/)
- titotal's critique: AI 2027's "superexponential" curve is empirically poorly justified, parameters bias toward early dates, code diverges from written methodology, at least six alternative narratives fit the same data, and "none of the AI2027 authors actually believe that 2027 is the median year" — [titotal](https://titotal.substack.com/p/a-deep-critique-of-ai-2027s-bad-timeline)

## KQ4. "AI can do each task" vs "the field's research is automated": bottlenecks and adoption lag

### Takeaway
Current evidence shows institutions, not model capability, setting the pace: journals and conferences are tightening rules on AI-written papers and desk-rejecting them at much higher rates (Organization Science's 2021-2026 audit: 69.6% desk rejection for heavily AI-written papers vs 43.7% for low-AI papers, secondary report), arXiv has blocked AI-generated surveys in CS, WRDS/Compustat access remains per-person, institution-gated and non-transferable with no AI-agent provisions found, and researcher adoption, while rising, was still "limited" in Wiley's ~5,000-researcher survey with fewer than 1 in 10 biomedical researchers using GenAI regularly in 2024.

### Cited Findings

**Journal / venue gatekeeping**
- Organization Science editors' task force analyzed nearly 7,000 submissions and 10,000+ reviews (2021-2026); a secondary summary reports 69.6% of papers classified 70%+ AI-written were desk rejected vs 43.7% of low-AI papers, and that heavy AI users (non-native-English teams, new entrants) "do not benefit in the review process and, in fact, may be hurt" (figures unverified against the editorial) — [University of Oregon news](https://business.uoregon.edu/news/ai-deluge-upending-academia)
- NeurIPS 2026 Position Paper track: 273 of 971 submissions (28.2%) flagged by Pangram as 100% AI; NeurIPS now requires position papers "substantially written by humans" (secondary coverage) — [36kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3999670422458247); [hyper.ai](https://hyper.ai/en/stories/0c6c363c26a30df235c7ca5549c8a04a)
- ICLR policy (26 Aug 2025) contemplates wholly AI-produced research with a responsible human author; ICML 2026 permits AI-assisted research; but an ICLR workshop chair says the main conference's rules do not allow purely AI-written papers — [takeoff.watch](https://takeoff.watch/questions/ai-writes-most-papers-at-top-ml-venue); [LessWrong on Zochi](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LtsgfGsXpiLTSGpaW/zochi-publishes-a-paper)
- arXiv blocked AI-generated survey/review papers in Computer Science after a "flood" — [Yahoo/404 Media](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/arxiv-blocks-ai-generated-survey-201452928.html)
- Submission volume: journals on ScholarOne received 33% more submissions in Q1 2026 than Q1 2025 (vendor blog) — [journalmetrics](https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/ai-manuscript-submission-surge-medical-journals-2026); desk rejection is manual and editor workloads "become unsustainable" as AI-boosted submissions double or triple — [arXiv 2509.16925](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.16925v1)
- Management-journal policies: AMJ editorial "From Scarcity to Abundance" (vol. 66, 2023; Grimes, Feuerriegel et al.) sees promise in synthesis/creation/evaluation/translation but says good-faith-based policies are inadequate and calls for specialized review protocols for AI-assisted papers — [Cambridge Judge](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2024/what-does-it-mean-to-be-a-scholar-in-an-age-of-ai/); JMS 2024 policy allows supervised, disclosed AI use but bars AI from peer review — [JMS editorial](https://business-society.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/J-Management-Studies-2024-Gatrell-Here-there-and-Everywhere-On-the-Responsible-Use-of-Artificial-Intelligence-AI-.pdf); SMJ guidelines (May 2025): GenAI allowed as assistant, not author; authors verify every citation — [SMJ](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/pb-assets/hub-assets/sms/AG/SMJ-Author-Instructions-May-2025-1748220403090.pdf)
- Sakana's accepted workshop paper was withdrawn because "the scientific community hasn't yet developed established standards for handling AI-generated manuscripts" — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-generated-paper-passes-peer-review-before-planned-withdrawal/)

**Data licensing / access**
- WRDS/Compustat: individual accounts approved by a local administrator; eligibility limited to current students/faculty/staff; use "limited to academic and non-commercial research purposes"; credential sharing prohibited; no provisions about LLMs or agents were found in any library or WRDS page surfaced — [Gothenburg library](https://databaser-lab.ub.gu.se/node/100516); [UChicago guide](https://guides.lib.uchicago.edu/wrds); [SFU record](https://databases.lib.sfu.ca/record/61245147700003610)
- Agents' weakest ReplicatorBench stage is data retrieval — [COS blog](https://www.cos.io/blog/benchmarking-llm-agents-on-scientific-tasks-introducing-replicatorbench)

**Adoption among researchers**
- Wiley survey of ~5,000 researchers (reported in Nature): current GenAI use "limited," researchers want guidance; 72% expect to expand use for manuscript preparation within two years, 67% for handling large information sets, 65% for accessibility/sharing — [Academica summary](https://academica.ca/top-ten/researchers-expect-ai-use-to-become-mainstream-need-more-training-to-expand-ai-use-study/)
- Ithaka S+R (2024 fieldwork): 6 in 10 biomedical researchers had ever used GenAI in research, 4 in 10 currently, fewer than 1 in 10 regularly — [Ithaka S+R](https://sr.ithaka.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Copy-of-Graphic-for-report.pdf)
- US STEM faculty (PLOS ONE 2025, n=232): 65% had used GenAI in teaching or research; 20% in both — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12393709/)
- 20-country survey of publishing academics (Mohammadi, Thelwall et al., IPM 2026): widespread awareness and adoption, large disciplinary/national differences; women less likely to use frequently; highest adoption in some non-Western countries — [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/298710445); perceptions tracked 2024-2026 "from novelty to normalisation" — [arXiv 2607.16223](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.16223)
- Anthropic published a post on coding agents in the social sciences including a survey of 1,260 quantitative social scientists (content not retrieved) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/coding-agents-social-sciences)
- Human-supervised use shows no reproduction-rate gain over humans alone (91% vs 94%) and misses more major coding errors — adoption does not automatically raise quality — [Brodeur et al.](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/43026/)

### Inferences
- "Each task automatable" is close to true for execution by Oct 2026; "field automated" is not, because the binding constraints are (1) license terms built around named human users, (2) journals that penalize detectable AI writing and have no standard for AI-generated manuscripts, and (3) slow, uneven researcher adoption. None of these move on METR's doubling clock; they move on policy and norm cycles measured in years.
- The Organization Science audit implies a near-term penalty, not a premium, for visibly AI-produced management papers — the opposite of a "pipeline edge" at the publication stage, unless the pipeline's output is indistinguishable and verified.
- Data access is the most defensible moat for an incumbent faculty member: institutional WRDS/Compustat/SEC/patent access plus the right to run agents on it is tied to the person's affiliation, not to the model.

- Primary text of the Organization Science editorial and the ASQ (Bechky & Davis 2025) editorial not retrieved; figures secondhand.
- No source on WRDS/S&P Global's stance toward LLM/agent querying of licensed data, nor on commercial connectors (e.g., Anthropic's financial-services connectors to S&P data) and whether academic licenses cover them — search budget exhausted before this query ran.
- No IRB-specific evidence found (less relevant to archival work, but not searched).
- No 2026 survey specific to management/strategy faculty adoption found.

## KQ5. Does the evidence support a "phases" model of shifting human advantage, and what is the durable human role?

### Takeaway
The data pattern is consistent with a phased shift: execution advantage is already thin (supervised AI ≈ human on reproduction; estimates "empirically consistent"), while advantage in ideation, mechanism/interpretation, verification against motivated reasoning, data access and institutional legitimacy is large and currently widening in relative terms (ideation is 71% of the econ gap; interpretation is the weakest audited sub-skill; journals penalize AI-written text). The commentators retrieved converge on "assistant, not co-scientist," with humans supplying direction, verification and accountability.

### Cited Findings
- Execution gap small, idea gap large: robustness shows no significant AI-human difference while idea quality (d=2.23) and mechanism analysis (d=1.43) do — [arXiv 2604.03338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03338)
- LLM-idea novelty premium disappears or reverses when ideas are executed by experts — [arXiv 2506.20803](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20803)
- Supervised AI matches humans on reproduction; autonomous AI does not (94/91/37%); authors say AI's value may lie where scale/cost preclude human review — [Brodeur et al.](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/43026/)
- Estimates stable under bias, verdicts not: the human role migrates from running models to owning the decision rule that turns estimates into claims — [arXiv 2606.11456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11456)
- Interpretation claims 57.9% accurate vs 85.5% for data analysis (Kosmos) — [arXiv 2511.02824](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02824)
- Virtual Lab and co-scientist designs keep a human providing high-level feedback or research goals — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/article/10.1038/s41586-025-09442-9); [Google](https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/)
- Zochi's A* acceptance still required humans to verify results and talk to reviewers — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LtsgfGsXpiLTSGpaW/zochi-publishes-a-paper)
- OpenAI's achieved Sept 2026 milestone is explicitly "under human direction" — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2251859/openai-says-it-reached-its-goal-of-creating-an-automated-research-intern/)
- Critic view: "a powerful assistant, but certainly not a co-scientist" until original, verifiable insight is shown — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/amp/ai-co-scientist-2674000043)
- Hassabis: domains needing physical experimentation lag coding/math — [Towards AI](https://pub.towardsai.net/what-amodei-and-hassabis-said-about-agi-timelines-jobs-and-china-at-davos-156308aa12c3)
- Management editors foresee AI value in synthesis, creation, evaluation and translation but demand new governance — [AMJ editorial via Cambridge Judge](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/2024/what-does-it-mean-to-be-a-scholar-in-an-age-of-ai/)
- Visible AI authorship is penalized at Organization Science (secondary) — legitimacy accrues to the accountable human — [University of Oregon](https://business.uoregon.edu/news/ai-deluge-upending-academia)
- Mass production of plausible papers is already feasible (Novy-Marx & Velikov), so scarcity shifts from papers to credible ideas and trusted authors — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33363)
- A 2026 framework paper argues the binding constraint on innovating with GenAI is the human (title only) — [arXiv 2608.07504](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07504)

### Inferences
- Phase 1 (now-~2028): edge from supervising execution well (verification, catching coding errors, choosing identification strategies beyond default DiD) and from having licensed data plus institutional standing. Evidence: Brodeur, Alizadeh, Ideation Bottleneck's 74% DiD monoculture.
- Phase 2 (~2028-2031, if lab timelines hold): execution fully commoditized; edge concentrates in idea selection, theoretical framing and the ability to certify results to skeptical gatekeepers. Evidence: idea gap is the largest and slowest-closing measured component; journal policies reward human accountability.
- Phase 3: edge in data access, field networks and legitimacy only, if models close the ideation gap. No evidence yet on when that happens for social science; the AI/ML analog (LLM ideas now beating humans ex ante but not ex post) suggests it is not imminent.

### Gaps
- No retrieved essays specifically on "research taste" as the durable human skill (planned search not run).
- No empirical study of whether AI narrows or widens productivity gaps between top and average social scientists; to my background knowledge the widely-cited MIT materials-science paper on this (Toner-Rodgers) was withdrawn in 2025 over data concerns — unverified this session.
- Korinek's own scenario thinking for economists' careers (he has written on this) was not retrieved.

## KQ6. Is the user's "mostly automated in ~10 years / shrinking edge for ~8 years" framing realistic, too slow, or too fast?

### Takeaway
On the evidence, the framing is too slow for execution (already mostly automatable under supervision in 2026, and the AI-assisted edge over human-only teams is currently near zero), roughly right-to-optimistic for idea generation and contribution judgment (the dominant gap, with no quantified social-science forecast), and probably too fast for "the field is automated" in the institutional sense (licensing, gatekeeping, adoption). The 8-year edge is plausible only if the edge is defined as supervision/ideation/data/legitimacy rather than as running a pipeline, because pipelines are commoditizing on a 4-7-month doubling clock while institutional constraints move on multi-year cycles.

### Cited Findings
- Execution near parity under supervision now (91% vs 94%) — [Brodeur et al.](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/43026/); estimates consistent across 20 agent runs — [arXiv 2606.11456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11456)
- Idea quality is the bottleneck (71%; 0.8% of AI papers beat median human on both) — [arXiv 2604.03338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03338)
- Capability clock: doubling ~89-131 days (TH1.1) with saturation near 16-17 hours — [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/); [FutureSearch](https://futuresearch.ai/app/p/a/frontier-metr-time-horizon-2029)
- Lab targets 2027-2028 for AI researchers "under human direction" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/28/sam-altman-says-openai-will-have-a-legitimate-ai-researcher-by-2028/); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2251859/openai-says-it-reached-its-goal-of-creating-an-automated-research-intern/)
- Institutional clock: heavy-AI papers desk-rejected at 69.6% vs 43.7% (secondary) — [University of Oregon](https://business.uoregon.edu/news/ai-deluge-upending-academia); fabricated-reference prevalence rising 10x in 3 years — [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/05/07/one-in-277-pubmed-indexed-papers-in-2026-shows-fabricated-references-says-analysis/); WRDS per-person access — [Gothenburg library](https://databaser-lab.ub.gu.se/node/100516)
- Mass-production precedent: ~288-400 finance papers in ~12 hours — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33363)

### Inferences
- "Mostly automated" needs a definition. If it means "most hours of a typical empirical project are done by AI under human direction," the field is arguably within 2-4 years, not 10. If it means "AI selects questions, executes, and publishes with minimal human input and journals accept it," 10 years is at the optimistic end given no social-science-specific forecast, gatekeeping trends, and the still-large ideation gap.
- The user's pipeline (lit synthesis over ~8-9k papers, question generation, variable construction, estimation, interpretation, drafting) maps onto exactly the stages where 2026 evidence is strongest (synthesis, construction, estimation) and weakest (question quality, interpretation). The edge from the strong stages is real but short-lived: these are commodity capabilities with a 4-7-month doubling clock and vendors (Edison, OpenAI, Anthropic) packaging them. The edge from the weak stages is the one worth cultivating.
- Risk asymmetry: pipelines that over-produce DiD designs with LLM-written theory sections and unverified citations are precisely what editors are learning to desk-reject; a "speed" strategy could reduce rather than increase hit rates at top management journals in 2026-2028.

### Gaps
- No forecast, survey, or market was found that puts a date on automation of social-science or management research specifically; this remains an inference from AI/ML forecasts plus institutional evidence.
