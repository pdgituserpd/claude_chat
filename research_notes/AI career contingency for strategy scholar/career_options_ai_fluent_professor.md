## Option 1: Baseline — lateral move up the research tier (R3 → R2 → R1) and what tenure actually protects

### Takeaway
The baseline plan trades a secure-but-flat $135K for a ~$155K+summer R2 line whose value depends on two things the 2023–2026 record shows are weakening: (a) tenure as job protection, which has been pierced repeatedly via program closures and financial-exigency declarations and is now legislatively conditioned in Ohio, Texas, Indiana and Florida; and (b) publication counts as the currency of advancement, which journals themselves report are being inflated by AI-assisted submissions. Higher research tiers remain better paid but concentrate exposure in exactly the asset (empirical publication output) most likely to be devalued.

### Inferences
- The R2 target ($155K + summer, 2-2) is roughly a 15–25% raise over $135K before summer money; against the SUNY Fredonia ($90–100K) and Grand Valley ($135K) postings, the user's current $135K is already at the upper end of the teaching-tier band, so the marginal gain from R2 is modest relative to the career risk being taken on.
- Tenure is now a conditional contract in at least four large states (OH, TX, IN, FL): statutory post-tenure review with for-cause termination pathways, plus demonstrated willingness of boards to use exigency/program-closure routes (WVU, Emporia, IIT, Columbia College Chicago). It protects against individual dismissal for speech far better than against institutional contraction.
- The Florida evidence (high performers exit under post-tenure review) suggests the people best placed to leave do leave; for a mobile AI-fluent scholar, tenure's option value is lower than for an immobile one.
- Higher research tiers are *more* exposed if AI devalues publication counts: their compensation, promotion and prestige are indexed to a metric now inflating (33% submission growth; Org Science surge in low-quality submissions), while R3/teaching lines are indexed to enrollment and teaching evaluations. Conversely, R1/R2 lines are *less* exposed to teaching disruption because research buyouts and smaller loads mean teaching is a smaller fraction of the value proposition.
- An AI pipeline that raises output mainly by volume is betting on the metric most at risk; pipelines that raise *quality* (novel data, replicable methods, infrastructure others reuse) are the defensible version of the baseline plan.

## Option 2: Teaching-track and executive-education positioning ("AI and strategy" / "AI for managers")

### Takeaway
Exec-ed revenue is at record levels at elite schools (HBS $253M FY2025) and AI-focused short programs are the growth product, but the verified premium accrues to institutions and star faculty; no survey evidence of a systematic pay premium for teaching-track faculty who can teach AI was found, and GMAC data show the executive/part-time MBA formats that employ such faculty shrinking 10–15% in 2025 even as full-time MBA rebounded. Teaching is less exposed than research to publication-count collapse but faces a credible AI-tutor substitution threat with RCT evidence.

### Inferences
- Exec-ed AI demand is real and institution-level revenue is verifiably at records, but the economics flow to schools with brands and to a small set of faculty who become the "face" of AI programs. For an R3 strategy professor, the realistic routes are (a) teaching in another school's open-enrollment or custom programs as an adjunct (fee per day; no survey data found), or (b) building an AI-for-managers course that becomes a local revenue line and a negotiating asset.
- The GMAC format data cut against the EMBA/part-time teaching play: the formats that most need "AI for managers" teaching capacity are the ones shrinking (10–15% in 2025), while full-time MBAs grew only 4%.
- Teaching is less exposed than research to AI-driven devaluation of publication counts, but it is not AI-safe: the Kestin RCT shows well-designed AI tutors can beat expert instruction on learning gains at the introductory level. The defensible teaching position is high-touch, high-fee (exec ed, custom programs, judgment-intensive strategy cases), not large-enrollment lecture courses.
- A teaching-track move typically means giving up tenure eligibility; given that tenure is itself being conditioned (Option 1), the cost of that trade is lower than it was in 2019, but teaching-track lines are also the first cut in program closures.

## Option 3: Industry transitions — corporate strategy, AI consulting, AI-lab economics/policy teams, enterprise AI roles

### Takeaway
AI-lab economist roles are the clearest verified pay step-up ($265K–$400K base at OpenAI/Anthropic in 2025–2026) and explicitly want PhD social scientists who can measure AI's economic effects; MBB hires PhDs at MBA-level pay (~$245–285K first-year total); corporate "head of AI" roles range enormously ($150K at a college to $1.6M median at Fortune-scale firms). Documented lab hires from business schools are almost all tenured, elite, on-leave economists (Chatterji, Jones, Korinek, Holtz), so the credential being bought is reputation plus economics skill, not AI tooling fluency alone.

## Cross-cutting: Which options reward AI "supervisor/taste/judgment" skills vs. credentials, and exposure matrix

### Takeaway
Judgment-and-taste skills are explicitly priced in lab economics roles (measurement design), exec-ed/custom teaching (translating AI for managers), and litigation/advisory work; MBB, corporate CAIO and administrative lines mostly price the credential and organizational seniority. The options least exposed to collapse of publication value are teaching/exec-ed, administration and industry; the options least exposed to teaching disruption are research-tier faculty with buyouts, lab roles, and litigation consulting; the only options reasonably hedged against both are lab/industry economics roles and portfolio strategies that keep one foot in each.

### Cited Findings
- Lab postings describe the work as building "new methods to measure the usage, diffusion, and impact of AI throughout the economy" (Anthropic Research Economist, Feb 2026) — [InferenceJobs](https://inferencejobs.com/companies/anthropic/jobs/research-economist-economic-research); OpenAI's economist role focuses on "AI's real-world economic impact" for ≤5-years-post-PhD economists — [startup.jobs](https://startup.jobs/economist-openai-8602936)
- Consulting firms are moving to outcome-based fees (≈25% of McKinsey fees) and AI is 25–30% of BCG/Bain revenue, i.e., buyers are paying for results, not analyst hours — [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/financial-accounting/boston-consulting-group-says-ai-work-brought-25-of-2025-revenue); [LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/consulting-firms-shift-toward-outcome-based-ai-fees-d0707ad1)
- Peer review is being flooded with low-effort AI submissions and reviews (Org Science, 2021–2026 data), which raises the value of editorial judgment and lowers the value of raw output — [LetsDataScience summary](https://letsdatascience.com/news/ai-generated-papers-overwhelm-academic-peer-review-63f4b506/)
- AI tutors beat expert active-learning instruction in an RCT (Kestin et al., 2025) — [ETC Journal review](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/)
- GMAC: executive/part-time formats −10–15% in 2025; full-time +4% — [GMAC](https://www.gmac.com/resources/learners/business-programs/explore-programs/students-prioritize-in-person-degrees-mba-applications-rise-again)
- Post-tenure review laws make tenured research faculty terminable for "does not meet expectations" ratings (Ohio) or productivity measures (Indiana) — [ORC 3345.453](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3345.453); [IU BOT-24](https://www.policies.iu.edu/policies/bot-24-post-tenure-faculty/index.html)

### Inferences
- Exposure matrix (inference from the above):
  - If AI collapses the value of empirical publication output: MOST exposed = R1/R2 research faculty and pure research-track positioning (Options 1, 6 as a volume play, 5 if grant-dependent). LEAST exposed = exec-ed/teaching (2), industry/lab roles (3), administration (7), litigation/advisory (4).
  - If AI also disrupts teaching (tutors, enrollment contraction): MOST exposed = teaching-track and large-enrollment MBA/EMBA teaching (2), and R3 lines whose value is teaching load. LEAST exposed = lab/industry economics (3), litigation consulting (4), senior administration (7), and R1 research lines with low teaching dependence — but those research lines are then exposed on the other axis.
  - Jointly least exposed: industry/lab roles that pay for measurement judgment, and a barbell that holds a tenured line while building outside income (see next section).
- Skills vs credentials: lab economist, exec-ed star, expert witness and methods-editor roles reward judgment (what to measure, how to design interventions, what counts as a good answer); MBB entry, CAIO postings and administrative appointments reward the PhD/professor title and seniority. The user's comparative advantage (agentic pipelines over firm-level data) is most legible to lab economic-research teams and to consultancies' AI practices, least legible to tenure committees that still count AIS-weighted publications.

### Gaps
- No empirical study found that quantifies how AI changes returns to publication counts in business-school labor markets; the exposure ranking is inference.

## Cross-cutting: Option-value / "barbell" strategies for AI-exposed academics

### Takeaway
No explicit advisory essay on a "barbell" for AI-exposed academics was located, but the institutional evidence points one way: elite faculty are exercising leave options rather than resigning, Stanford formalizes a 2-in-7 private-sector leave, lab advisory roles are often uncompensated reputation plays, and post-tenure review laws make tenure a weaker hedge in several states — so the implicit expert consensus is "hold the academic seat as long as it is cheap to hold, build outside legibility, and move only via reversible steps."

### Cited Findings
- Stanford's formal policy: tenured faculty may take two years of unpaid leave every seven years to work in the private sector; Stanford says professors often return — [The News (BI relay)](https://www.thenews.com.pk/latest/1417711-stanford-gsb-dean-admits-i-worry-about-losing-faculty-to-ai)
- Chatterji, Jones, Korinek, Holtz and Nelson all moved via leave rather than resignation (2024–2026) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Chatterji); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/anthropic-hires-chad-jones-ai-risks/); [PIIE](https://www.piie.com/index%2Ephp/experts/senior-research-staff/anton-korinek); [Digg](https://digg.com/ai/bkotxle9); [Digg](https://digg.com/tech/1r7064v5)
- Korinek's Anthropic council role was uncompensated (Darden, June 2025) — [Darden](https://news.darden.virginia.edu/2025/06/11/uva-darden-professor-to-help-assess-ais-economic-impact-on-new-council/)
- Florida post-tenure review increased exits of high-performing researchers without raising productivity (Quach & Yu, 2025) — [TaxProf Blog](https://taxprofblog.aals.org/2025/06/01/2025_06_0449_fight-or-flight-the-impact-of-post-tenure-evaluations-on-faculty-productivity-and-selection/)
- A faculty side business reached ~half of salary (2017 Chronicle essay) — [Chronicle](https://www.chronicle.com/article/How-to-Make-Money-From/241365); older debate on whether outside consulting is consistent with tenure's purpose — [Harvard Crimson, 1973](https://api.thecrimson.com/article/1973/3/28/the-abuse-of-tenure-pto-the)
- AACSB: deans' top priority is AI-workforce preparation (2025) — a demand signal a faculty member can serve from inside — [P&Q Undergrads](https://poetsandquantsforundergrads.com/?p=166756)
- Lab credit programs are small (Anthropic ≤$20K; OpenAI ≤$1K) but provide external legibility — [Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11199177); [OpenAI](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10139500-researcher-access-program-faq)

### Inferences
- A defensible barbell for this profile: (1) stay on a tenure-track line (pursue the R2 move only if the destination state lacks statutory post-tenure review and the school is not tuition-fragile), (2) direct the AI pipeline at *quality and public legibility* — open measurement tools, replicable firm-level datasets, Org Science/SMJ-targeted AI-and-strategy papers — rather than paper count, (3) build the outside leg through exec-ed/AI-for-managers teaching and advisory/contract research, and (4) treat a lab or consulting move as a leave-based, reversible step timed to the tenure decision or a verified offer.
- Timing: the AI-and-strategy special-issue window (2024–2026) and the lab economics-hiring wave (2025–2026 postings) are both live now; the tenure-protection erosion is also accelerating (OH 2025, TX 2025, IN revisions Dec 2025, FY2027 SBE threat), which argues for building the outside leg within the next 2–3 years rather than after tenure.

### Gaps
- No career-advice essays or economist commentary specifically on barbell/option-value strategies for AI-exposed academics were found (search budget exhausted before targeted queries on Cowen, Mollick, Noah Smith or Chronicle advice columns could run).
- No data on university consulting-day policies or outside-income caps at R2/R3 business schools.
