# US Business-School Academic Labor Market for Strategy/Management Faculty (as of October 2026): Economics of Moving from a Teaching-Oriented Regional to a "Balanced" R2 / Lower-R1

Research-method note: all findings below come from web-search result summaries (roughly 45 searches). Direct fetching of primary documents (AACSB PDFs, Springfield Daily Citizen articles, Missouri State policy PDFs, CUPA-HR tables) was blocked at the network layer in this session, and the shared search budget was exhausted before every question could be closed. Figures that came only from a search summary of a document I could not open are marked "(reported; document not opened)". Year of each data point is labeled.

## Key Question 0: What is Missouri State University, institutionally? (Carnegie classification check)

### Takeaway
Missouri State is not an "R3" in any current formal sense: under the redesigned 2025 Carnegie system it received the new "Research Colleges and Universities" (RCU) designation, the tier below R2, reserved for institutions spending >$2.5M/yr on research that do not meet the doctoral-production thresholds for R1/R2.

### Cited Findings
- Missouri State was one of 216 institutions placed in the new 2025 Carnegie "Research Colleges and Universities" category; the category covers schools that spend more than $2.5 million on research annually but do not meet the doctorate-granting benchmarks for R1 or R2 (2025) — [Missouri State News, Mar 3 2025](https://news.missouristate.edu/2025/03/03/missouri-state-joins-new-carnegie-classification/)
- MSU reported a record $63.2 million in external research funding for FY2024 (second straight record year) — [Missouri State News, Mar 3 2025](https://news.missouristate.edu/2025/03/03/missouri-state-joins-new-carnegie-classification/)
- Not to be confused with Missouri S&T (UM System), which received R1 status in the 2025 classification — [Missouri S&T News, Feb 2025](https://econnection.mst.edu/2025/02/missouri-st-earns-highest-research-designation-from-carnegie-foundation/)
- MSU's revenue mix: tuition and fees about 59% of income, state funding about 38% (2025-26 reporting) — [Springfield Daily Citizen](https://sgfcitizen.org/schools/springfield-colleges-universities/enrollment-impacts-msu-budget/)

### Inferences
- "R3" is informal shorthand (it last existed as "Doctoral Universities: Moderate Research Activity" in the 2015 scheme). For benchmarking purposes MSU should be compared to AACSB master's/doctoral-professional regionals and the new RCU cohort, not to R2s.
- Because tuition is ~59% of revenue, MSU's faculty-compensation capacity is far more enrollment-sensitive than at flagship R1s with larger research and state shares.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Carnegie 2025 institutional-classification page to confirm MSU's parallel "institutional classification" label (e.g., Master's/Doctoral-Professional) or its 2021 label.

## Key Question 1: Salary benchmarks for assistant/associate professors of management/strategy by tier (AACSB, CUPA-HR, AAUP, public databases). Is $135K high for a regional? Is $155K realistic at an R2?

### Takeaway
$135K on a 9-month contract is roughly at or a bit below the all-discipline AACSB mean for assistant professors ($134K in 2024-25 data; ~$156K mean / ~$147K median in 2025-26 data) but is clearly healthy for a regional RCU; $155K base for a strategy assistant/associate at a balanced R2 is plausible (an R2-turned-R1's 2024 approved median for management assistant professors was ~$145K; AACSB 2025-26 assistant mean ~$156K; R1 postings run $190K-$230K) but is at the optimistic end for an R2 new hire and more realistic for an associate-level or very productive lateral.

### Inferences
- Is $135K high for a regional? Relative to all-business, all-rank national means (~$115-117K, 2023-25) and the 2017 strategy-assistant mean ($118.7K), $135K is strong. Relative to the AACSB assistant-professor mean across all accredited schools (which R1 and private salaries pull up), it is roughly at the 2024-25 mean and ~13% below the 2025-26 mean / ~8% below the 2025-26 median. Verdict: high for a 3-3 regional RCU, average for AACSB overall. Strategy historically carries a ~10% premium over general management (2017: $118.7K vs $108.1K), which helps explain the number.
- Is $155K realistic at an R2? The best public R2-ish datapoint (UNC Charlotte's 2024 approved median for management assistant professors, ~$145K) plus the 2025-26 AACSB assistant mean (~$156K) put $150-160K inside the plausible range for a strategy hire at a well-funded public R2 in 2026-27, especially with 2 years of general inflation since the UNC Charlotte table. It is less plausible at under-resourced R2s and at R2s in low-cost-of-living states; it is conservative for an R1 (MSU-East Lansing posts $190-230K).
- Real-pay erosion matters: with AAUP showing real salaries still 6.2% below 2019 and MSU granting a flat $600 raise one year and zero the next (see Key Question 5), the gap between a frozen $135K and a market-reset $155K widens every year the person stays.

## Key Question 2: Summer research support (1/9, 2/9, 3/9) and publication incentives by tier; what they total at a $155K base; guaranteed vs. contingent

### Takeaway
Arithmetically, 2/9ths on $155K is $34,444 (1/9 = $17,222; 3/9 = $51,667), so a true 2/9ths package would push total pay to ~$189K, not $170K; but I found no evidence that permanent 2/9ths is "typical" at R2 business schools; published norms describe summer stipends of one to three months, usually for a fixed number of early years and/or contingent on productivity, and documented R2 publication incentives are in the $2,000-$5,000-per-top-article range (vs. MSU's $2,000 per peer-reviewed article in any journal with acceptance rate ≤50%).

### Cited Findings
Ninths: definitions, caps and norms
- "Ninths" convention: 1/9 = one month of academic-year salary; summer salary is commonly negotiated for one or two years, sometimes up to three, and is not a permanent part of the contract (general academia, not business-specific) — [Chronicle, "Explaining the 9-month contract"](https://www.chronicle.com/article/explaining-the-9-month-contract)
- AOM Career Center (2026): departments and schools that provide summer research stipends commonly pay "one to three months of equivalent salary"; some schools weight FT50/UTD24 publications, grants and editorial roles in annual merit reviews — [AOM Career Center, Research Support and Funding for Management Faculty Teams (2026)](https://career-center.aom.org/career-resources/finding-talent-5/research-support-and-funding-for-management-faculty-teams-2026-49)
- AACSB (Dec 2022): business schools still offer new hires guaranteed research funds and teaching-load reductions, but use of both benefits declined over the preceding five years (2017-2022) — [AACSB, Trends in Faculty Engagement and Compensation](https://www.aacsb.edu/insights/articles/2022/12/trends-in-faculty-engagement-and-compensation)
- NSF's "two-ninths rule" caps federally funded summer salary at 2/9 of academic-year base — [Ferris State grants FAQ](https://www.ferris.edu/administration/academicaffairs/vpoffice/Academic_Research/grantsupport/faq/Fac_compensation.htm); Amherst caps grant-funded summer salary at 3/9 — [Amherst grants office](https://www.amherst.edu/offices/grants/preparing-a-grant-proposal/building-a-budget/calculating-salaries-for-summer-faculty-undergrad-student-researchers-postbac-and-postdoc)
- University caps (all fields): UCLA total summer salary from all sources ≤3/9, with Vice Chancellor approval required above 2/9 on state funds — [UCLA APO summer policy](https://apo.ucla.edu/compensation/summer-policy/policy-and-procedure-for-summer-employment); Georgia Tech ceiling of one-third of 9-month pay — [Georgia Tech Faculty Handbook 3.3.2](https://policylibrary.gatech.edu/faculty-handbook/3.3.2-salary-determinations-tenure-track-faculty); Harvard FAS ≤3/9 from Harvard sources — [Harvard FAS compensation](https://academic-appointments.fas.harvard.edu/b-compensation); Cornell CALS "normal" 2/9-2.5/9 from grants, with requests above 2.5/9 no longer approved starting Summer 2027 — [Cornell CALS](https://cals.cornell.edu/faculty-staff/academic-policies/beyond-basics/summer-salary-for-tenure-track-faculty); CU Boulder grant-funded summer capped at 1/9 per summer in one excerpt and 3/9 total in another — [CU Boulder Faculty Affairs](https://www.colorado.edu/facultyaffairs/faculty-personnel-actions-career-milestones/evaluation-compensation/additional-compensation)
- Georgetown's internal Summer Salary Supplement caps total summer support at 2/9 of academic-year salary and explicitly excludes McDonough School of Business faculty — [Georgetown Provost](https://provost.georgetown.edu/main-campus-research/internal-research-funding-and-awards/summer-salary-supplement/)
- Regional AACSB comparator in Missouri: UCM's Harmon College of Business provides a summer research stipend "subject to funding availability" (policy document dated 2014) — [UCM Harmon College Guide PDF](https://www.ucmo.edu/offices/general-counsel/university-policy-library/academic-policies/promotion-and-tenure-college-guidelines/harmon-college-guide-to-policies-and-procedures.pdf)
- R1 business-school example (reported; document not opened): UF Warrington's faculty research award for tenured/tenure-track faculty is the greater of 2/9 of nine-month salary or $35,000, capped at $80,000, paid as a lump sum and tied to college funding availability (2025 guidelines) — [UF Warrington research award guidelines (docx)](https://warrington.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/research-award-guidelines-commitment.docx); [Warrington awards page](https://warrington.ufl.edu/faculty-and-staff/awards/). A follow-up search for the "$35,000" and "2/9" terms did not re-confirm these figures, so treat as unverified. A 2009-10 Warrington committee report described ~27 summer research grants of $25,000+ awarded from 35 applicants (2010) — [Warrington Research Annual Report 2009 PDF](https://warrington.ufl.edu/faculty-and-staff/wp-content/uploads/sites/55/2018/04/Research-AnnualReport-2009.pdf)
- Missouri State's own university-level programs: Faculty Research Grant up to $7,500 and Summer Faculty Fellowship $6,000 stipend via the Graduate College (2025-26) — [MSU Faculty Research Grants](https://graduate.missouristate.edu/FacultyStaff/FacultyResearchGrants.htm); [MSU Summer Faculty Fellowships](https://graduate.missouristate.edu/facultystaff/SummerFellowships.htm); MSU's College of Education has a separate "Summer Research Support Policy for New Faculty" — [MSU COE](https://education.missouristate.edu/summer-research-support-policy.htm)

Publication incentives by tier

### Inferences
- Arithmetic on the user's scenario (not sourced; computed): $155,000 base × 2/9 = $34,444 (total ~$189.4K); × 1/9 = $17,222 (total ~$172.2K); × 3/9 = $51,667 (total ~$206.7K). The user's "$170K+" is consistent with ~1/9 or a fixed ~$15K summer research stipend, not with 2/9ths. If a school really offers 2/9ths, the total should be stated as ~$190K; if the offer is ~$170K total, the summer component is ~1/9.
- "Typical 2/9ths at an R2" is unverified and probably overstated as a permanent entitlement. Evidence points to: (a) summer research money at research-oriented schools is usually 1-3 months, (b) most commonly guaranteed only for the first 2-3 years of an appointment and thereafter contingent on productivity or on a competitive internal process, and (c) AACSB itself reports guaranteed research funds for new hires declining since 2017. A lateral mover should negotiate the number of guaranteed summers explicitly and ask what the renewal criteria are.
- Publication awards: "larger awards at an R2" is directionally right for top-tier hits (BGSU pays $5,000 vs. MSU's $2,000) but the per-article difference is modest (~$3K per FT50 paper), and MSU's program is actually broader (any ≤50%-acceptance peer-reviewed journal qualifies). For a scholar producing one FT50-level paper per year, the incentive difference is a rounding error relative to base salary and summer-support differences.
- Note that MSU's $2,000 is formally labeled a "summer research grant" per article — i.e., it is the College's summer-research mechanism, not a pure bonus; the user's framing ("$2,000 per top-tier article") understates its reach but correctly captures its size.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifies the share of R2 vs. R1 business schools offering 1/9, 2/9 or 3/9 summer support, or how many years are guaranteed; AACSB's Staff Compensation and Demographics Survey asks about supplemental pay but I could not open it.
- The UF Warrington figures (2/9 or $35K, cap $80K) came only from one search summary and could not be verified.
- No public example was found of a US business school paying $10,000-$20,000 per A-journal article; such amounts may exist (often as "summer research support tied to A-hits") but did not surface in search results.

## Key Question 3: Typical teaching loads by tier (3-3/4-4 at teaching schools, 2-2 at balanced, 1-1/2-1 at research) and how enrollment pressure is changing them

### Takeaway
Documented loads match the user's mental model at the ends of the spectrum (R1 UMass Lowell: 1-1 → 2-1 → 2-2; regional MSU: 3-3) but not reliably in the middle: some R2 business schools (UMass Dartmouth) advertise 3-3 for tenure-track management, Stevens assigns new assistant professors three courses per year and tenured publication-active faculty 3-4 per year, and AACSB sets no load standard, using 3-3 as its own illustrative peer benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- AACSB standards do not specify teaching loads; peer-review teams benchmark against the school's peer list, e.g., "if the peer schools expect a 3-3 teaching load and the school being reviewed has a 5-4, this may be deemed to be too high" — [AACSB Faculty Qualifications FAQ](https://www.aacsb.edu/educators/accreditation/business-accreditation/resources/faculty-qualifications)
- AACSB 2020 standards: participating faculty must deliver at least 75% of teaching globally and 60% within each discipline (faculty sufficiency) — [AACSB 2020 Interpretive Guidance, July 2021](https://www.aacsb.edu/-/media/documents/accreditation/2020-interpretive-guidance-july-2021.pdf)
- UMass Lowell Manning School (R1) tenure-track management: standard load 1:1 in the first two years, 2:1 in the next two, 2:2 thereafter, with course reductions available for top-journal publications (2025-26 posting for 2026 start) — [UMass careers, Assistant Professor - Management (Tenure-Track)](https://careers.umass.edu/cw/en-us/job/528694/assistant-professor-management-tenuretrack)
- UMass Dartmouth Charlton College (R2) tenure-track management: three sections per semester (3-3) of undergraduate and/or graduate courses, plus research expectations (2025-26 posting) — [UMass careers, Assistant Professor - Management](https://careers.umass.edu/cw/en-us/job/528943/assistant-professor-management)
- Stevens Institute School of Business (private R2) workload policy AY2024-25: publication-active tenured faculty normally carry three to four courses per year; lower loads require "exemplary publishing at the highly active level," funded research or extra service; new tenure-track assistant professors with <3 years' experience carry three courses per year during the initial four-year appointment (2024) — [Stevens Workload Policy AY2024-25 PDF](https://assets.stevens.edu/mviowpldu823/57BXRVFPDMw8e3tNrL9Amw/89c8fc1ecff3ed7fcb609f43cbdfc79e/Workload_Policy_AY2024-25_updated_2.6.2024.pdf)
- BGSU (R2) offers a course release as the alternative to the $5,000 cash award for a UTD24/FT50/4* publication (2024-25) — [BGSU Research Incentive Program](https://www.bgsu.edu/business/faculty-and-staff/faculty-professional-development-research-incentive-program.html)
- AACSB (2022): use of teaching-load reductions as a new-hire benefit declined 2017-2022 — [AACSB, Dec 2022](https://www.aacsb.edu/insights/articles/2022/12/trends-in-faculty-engagement-and-compensation)

Tuition-dependent Midwestern regionals like MSU are on the wrong side of each of those redistributions; large public R2/R1 business schools in growing states are on the right side.
- For the user, the asymmetry is stark: MSU's compensation trajectory (flat $600, then $0, with state core funding frozen and a projected FY2028 state shortfall) implies a real-terms pay cut of roughly inflation each year with no overload/summer growth, whereas a market-rate move resets base salary in one step. The dominant financial variable over 5-10 years is probably the base-salary reset and the cadence of raises at the destination, not publication bonuses.
- Downside risks at the destination: R2s in Midwest/Northeast states face the same demographics; hiring freezes in 2025 hit even elite units; MBA programs (where strategy faculty often teach) are consolidating toward large full-time or online programs and specialized master's. Strategy faculty who can teach undergraduate capstone, specialized-master's and online formats are better positioned than MBA-only teachers.
